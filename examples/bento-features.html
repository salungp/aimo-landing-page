What AIMO actually does,
in six tiles.

Copy and illustration concepts for the feature bento. Every tile pairs one claim with one moving picture of that claim — no stock icons, no screenshots. Sandbox only; nothing here is wired into the app.

Gasless

No gas token. No stuck trade.

Network fees are sponsored, so you never hold a chain's coin just to move on it.

Trade$250.00
Network fee$2.41

You pay$250.00

IllustrationA receipt that edits itself. The fee line strikes through, then the sponsored line lifts in underneath and the total never moves — the whole claim in one four-second loop, no words needed. Cheaper alternative if the loop reads gimmicky: a static receipt with the fee already struck. Do not animate the total; a moving total reads as a price change.

Spot

Buy it. Hold it. It's yours.

Real assets in a wallet that only does spot, so long-term holdings stay clear of whatever you're trading today.

  • ETH$2,410
  • BTC$1,780

IllustrationA holdings ladder, not a price chart. Spot is about ownership, so the picture is allocation — four bars, stablecoin dimmed so it reads as the resting slot. Bars breathe a few percent on a long offset loop, enough to feel live, not enough to look like a ticker. A candle chart here would say "trading", which is the perps tile's job.

Perps

Leverage, kept in its own lane.

Size, margin and liquidation all live in the Perps wallet. A bad hour there never reaches the rest of your money.

The knob drifting between 2× and 10× is the interactive bit of the product shown at rest — and the liquidation price sitting in the legend is what makes "its own lane" concrete. On the real card make the track draggable and let the PnL respond; it is the one tile where interaction beats animation.

Prediction

Trade what you think happens next.

Take a side on real events at live odds, funded from a wallet you set aside for it — so a conviction bet and your portfolio never share a balance.

IllustrationA market card with odds that drift. The split bar is the whole idea: two sides, priced, always summing to one. It drifts a few points on a slow loop so the word "live" is earned. Wide tile on purpose — it closes the bento and gives the question room to be a real sentence, which is what sells prediction markets to someone who has never traded one.
